Release v3.16.0. The spike credibility gate is scored on each region's own interconnectors rather than QNI for everyone, its threshold is calibrated and found not to be identifiable, and the flag is no longer published inside 24 hours where the calibration shows it selects worse than the raw forecast.

## A Queensland link was answering for five regions

The network covariate behind `spike_credible` read QNI regardless of which region was being forecast (#176). QNI is the Queensland to New South Wales link. For VIC1, SA1 and TAS1 it is not an interconnector those regions sit on, so the gate was scoring a scarcity signal from somewhere else and treating it as local. NSW1 and QLD1 were right for the wrong reason, since QNI happens to be one of their links but not the only one.

Each region is now scored on the interconnectors it actually sits on, taken from AEMO's own interconnector identifiers and the region at each end, with the direction handled per link so an import into the region counts as an import whichever way the link is signed. VIC1 gains VIC1-NSW1, V-SA, V-S-MNSP1 and T-V-MNSP1; SA1 gains V-SA and V-S-MNSP1; TAS1 gains T-V-MNSP1; NSW1 gains VIC1-NSW1, NSW1-QLD1 and TERRANORA alongside QNI; QLD1 gains TERRANORA alongside QNI. The gate takes the tightest link in the region's set rather than an average, so one constrained import path is enough to register.

## The threshold is a chosen constant, not a fitted one

`SPIKE_CAPABILITY_DEPRESSION` stays at 0.25 and `const.py` now says plainly that it is chosen. The calibration behind that statement is in `docs/spike_threshold_calibration.md`.

`forecast_history` could not support the work. The calibration store persists `qni_mwflow` and `qni_violation` and nothing else about the network, so neither export limits nor import limits nor any per-region breakdown can be reconstructed from what the integration has written down. The calibration was rebuilt from AEMO's PD7DAY monthly archive instead: 817 daily 18:00 runs from June 2024 to September 2026, 105,620 forecast intervals at or above $3000/MWh, of which 1,314 settled there, a base rate of 1.24 per cent. The vectorised gate was checked against `network_covariates_for_interval` on 40 sampled intervals with no mismatches, so it calibrates this code rather than a paraphrase of it.

Every threshold between 0.05 and 0.50 gives the same precision to within bootstrap noise resampled by run. At 0.25 the 95 per cent interval runs from 1.44 to 2.71 per cent and contains the point estimate of every other value in that range. The best value also reverses between periods, and spike rate is not monotonic in the capability ratio, which is the assumption a threshold rule rests on. The area under the curve for the ratio is 0.526 overall and 0.369 in VIC1, where it is inverted.

## The flag is withheld inside 24 hours

Inside 24 hours the gate selects a worse subset than the raw forecast alone: 272 intervals flagged at 15.81 per cent precision against a 20.42 per cent base rate over the same band. In QLD1 it flagged three intervals inside 24 hours and got none of them right. Below `SPIKE_COVARIATE_MIN_HORIZON_H`, now 24 hours, sensor attributes report `spike_credible` as `none`, meaning no opinion rather than not credible, which is the tri-state consumers already handle. A forecast with no horizon is not treated as short lead.

The suppression sits in the sensor layer, not in `CalibrationStore.apply_to_price`. Putting it in the store switched off the entire 0 to 24 hour camera callout band, because callouts require the flag to be true and only exist inside 48 hours. The callout path therefore still reads the raw gate result and its behaviour is unchanged, pinned by tests in both directions. The flag consequently means slightly different things in the two places it is read. That is deliberate and recorded, taken because the alternative was changing what the chart draws on the strength of an analysis that was not scoped to callouts. Inside 24 hours the callouts are still selected by a rule this work found worse than the raw forecast, which is left open rather than quietly changed.

`SPIKE_COVARIATE_BYPASS_HORIZON_H` is retired. It belonged to the removed capping path and was never read.

## What to expect on deploy

- `spike_credible` and the network covariate attributes change value on VIC1, SA1 and TAS1 entries, which were previously scored on a link those regions do not sit on.
- `spike_credible` reports `none` on forecast intervals less than 24 hours ahead. Anything treating `none` as false keeps working; anything treating it as true does not.
- The forecast camera and its spike callouts are unchanged.
- No new entities.

## A correction

Work in progress on this change reported a 5.0 times lift in QLD1 and described the rule as close to useful there. That does not survive a Queensland-only breakdown: the figure rests on three spiked intervals out of 46 flagged, with a bootstrap interval from 0.00 to 19.42 per cent, and every one of those events falls between June 2024 and March 2025. The claim is withdrawn. Even at its best the gate turns a 1.24 per cent chance of a spike into about 2.2 per cent, so a credible flag still means no spike roughly 49 times out of 50.

Suite goes from 1204 to 1246 tests.
